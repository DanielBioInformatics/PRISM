Methodology Summary

PRISM computes a comprehensive panel of physicochemical descriptors from a user-provided amino acid sequence using the BioPython ProtParam library (Cock et al., 2009). Input sequences are filtered to the 20 standard amino acids; sequences shorter than two residues are rejected. The following parameters are calculated: molecular weight (in daltons), isoelectric point (pI) via the method of Bjellqvist et al. (1993), grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY; Kyte & Doolittle, 1982), aromaticity index (Lobry, 1997), and the instability index (Guruprasad et al., 1990).

Secondary structure fractions (α-helix, β-sheet, and loop/turn) are predicted from amino-acid propensities (Deleage & Roux, 1987). Molar extinction coefficients at 280 nm are calculated for both reduced and oxidised (disulfide-bonded) states using the Gill & von Hippel (1989) method. Amino-acid residue distributions are reported across five physicochemical groups (hydrophobic, positively charged, negatively charged, polar uncharged, and special/structural).

All computed values are interpreted through a rule-based drug-target strategy framework: GRAVY thresholds classify proteins as hydrophobic transmembrane, amphipathic, or hydrophilic; pI ranges determine electrostatic complementarity (acidic, neutral, or basic); and the instability index distinguishes stable (index < 40) from conformationally flexible targets. The complete analysis executes in under two seconds for a typical 300-residue sequence.
